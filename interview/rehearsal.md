# Rehearsal and ownership checklist

This is a written practice plan. No live rehearsal, timed speech or candidate scoring has been performed.

## Practice sequence

1. **Understand the story:** explain the commercial question without mentioning software. Read the two-minute story aloud, time it and rewrite it in your own words. Do not rush to fit an assumed speaking rate.
2. **Recall the evidence:** reconstruct the revenue boundary, monthly member bridge, three behavioural channels and portfolio scope from memory. Check the reference sheet afterwards.
3. **Defend methods:** answer one question from each of the six core topics. Follow each with the technical probe and explain one limitation without looking at the model answer.
4. **Defend decisions:** compare Hull, Portsmouth and Reading. Explain why positive modelled revenue does not automatically qualify a site or match it into the experiment.
5. **Face challenge:** take one prompt from each stakeholder role. Give a short answer, concede what is unknown and specify the next decision or evidence request.
6. **Demonstrate artifacts:** rehearse the five-minute navigation using saved files. Be able to explain a SQL denominator and a spreadsheet bridge. Use screenshots if native applications are unavailable.
7. **Stress-test understanding:** explain the simulated A/A MODIFY result, small-sample power and twelve-month STOP. State what would change with real contribution costs or competitor cuts without inventing an updated forecast.

## Answer-quality rubric

Score yourself from 0 to 3 on each dimension after practice. This is a learning aid, not a hiring predictor or a recorded result.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial relevance | Cannot identify the decision | Lists metrics | Connects behaviour to revenue/member cost | Prioritises the decision, conditions and next action |
| Evidence accuracy | Invents or misstates evidence | Recalls a number without scope | Correct value, population and window | Explains numerator, denominator and source |
| Method understanding | Cannot explain the method | Repeats terminology | Explains calculation and why it was chosen | Handles a follow-up or counterexample |
| Uncertainty and limits | Claims certainty or realised impact | Uses a generic caveat | Identifies the material limitation | Explains how the limitation changes a decision |
| Communication | Unclear or lengthy | Partly answers the question | Concise answer with a clear example | Adapts depth to interviewer and invites the relevant probe |
| Ownership | Invents personal work | Vague about contribution | Accurately describes assistance and contribution | Can demonstrate the work personally understood and checked |

Do not average away a serious accuracy or ownership issue. Revisit any answer that treats modelled revenue as earned profit, annualises monthly retention without support, sums interval endpoints or describes simulation as independent evidence.

## Ownership prompts to complete truthfully

- Which commercial decisions did I personally choose or challenge? What changed because of my input?
- Which scripts, formulas or visuals were AI-assisted? Which can I explain or reproduce independently?
- Which reconciliations did I personally inspect? What evidence proves they passed, and what did I learn?
- Did I personally operate Power BI Desktop, or am I citing the project's native acceptance record?
- Which result surprised me? Can I explain it from the source evidence rather than memorised notes?
- What would I do differently with real operator data, a Finance partner and customer research?

Suggested structure: **“I used [actual assistance] for [actual implementation]. I personally [actual contribution]. I checked [actual check] and learned [actual lesson]. I would still need [actual next evidence].”** Leave unperformed contributions out. The project record supports artifact facts, not invented personal experience.

## Concepts to demonstrate before interviewing

- [ ] Define opening, average and closing members, and reconcile the monthly bridge.
- [ ] Separate churn, same-month exits and tier transfers.
- [ ] Explain gross consumer prices, net revenue, subscription yield and total ARPMM.
- [ ] Distinguish descriptive headroom from local historical pricing evidence.
- [ ] Explain equal-pair log acquisition comparisons and why elasticity is not a whole-base loss rate.
- [ ] Explain notice, protected billing and one-time increments.
- [ ] Explain shared-draw portfolio uncertainty and unsupported selection scopes.
- [ ] Apply caution precedence, qualification gates and repeat-repricing treatment.
- [ ] Explain confidence-bound harms, early reviews and the primary versus exploratory experiment.
- [ ] Explain why A/A known paths validate the pipeline but cannot replace a treatment/control estimator.
- [ ] Discuss contribution, bad debt, LTV and competitor reactions as remaining evidence needs.
- [ ] Describe your own contribution honestly and identify what you still need to learn.

Live mock practice can assess these skills separately, one question at a time. Completion of this checklist has not been asserted.
